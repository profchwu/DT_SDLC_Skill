# Design Thinking

Run a conversational discovery workflow. Ask no more than three focused questions at a time. Separate confirmed facts, assumptions, and open questions. Never treat silence as approval.

## Adapt discovery depth

Extract what the user already supplied before asking questions. Check whether the goal, users and context, inputs, processing or decisions, outputs, constraints, AI role or authority, success measures, and acceptance examples are sufficiently clear for the next decision. Do not ask the user to repeat known information.

Choose the lightest adequate depth:

- **simple and low risk**: state a concise interpretation and assumptions, then ask only for a blocking correction or approval;
- **moderate or ambiguous**: ask one to three questions about gaps that materially change scope, experience, architecture, or acceptance;
- **complex or high risk**: resolve gaps progressively, prioritizing safety, data, external integrations, irreversible actions, and regulatory constraints.

Prefer a recommended default over a long option list. Skip questions whose answers can be safely inferred or deferred. Reassess depth when new complexity or risk appears.

## Workflow

1. **Empathize** — identify users, context, jobs, pain points, evidence, constraints, and affected stakeholders.
2. **Define** — synthesize a problem statement, scope, non-goals, success measures, and risks.
3. **Ideate** — produce multiple options, compare desirability, feasibility, viability, and risk, then recommend a direction.
4. **Prototype** — choose the cheapest artifact that can test the riskiest assumption.
5. **Test** — define participants, tasks, observations, measures, and a decision rule.

At the end of each stage, present:

- what is confirmed;
- what remains assumed or unknown;
- the artifact produced;
- a choice to approve, revise, or pause.

Do not advance past Define in guided mode until the user approves the problem and MVP boundary.

## Handoff

Create a project brief from `assets/project-brief-template.md`. Include target users, problem statement, evidence, MVP, non-goals, constraints, success metrics, assumptions, risks, acceptance examples, and unresolved questions. Mark the handoff `approved` only after explicit user confirmation.
